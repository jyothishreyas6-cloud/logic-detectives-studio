FOR GROWN-UPS
Learning goal: recognise how many are in a small group with one quick look, without counting (subitising), using the rule "You can see small groups without counting." (The ladybird has two dots on each wing: two and two make four, seen at a glance. Touching and counting each dot only once proves it.)
Curriculum link: NSW Mathematics K-10 Syllabus (2022), Early Stage 1 (Kindergarten), Representing whole numbers, MAE-RWN-01: "demonstrates an understanding of how whole numbers indicate quantity". https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/learning-areas/mathematics/mathematics-k-10-2022/outcomes
Ask your child after watching: "How did Ivy know there were four dots so fast?"
Try it together: roll a dice and say the number as fast as you can, without counting the dots.

"Hey! That's not fair! Ivy didn't count!"

WHAT YOUR CHILD WILL LEARN
- At a glance means one quick look, like the dots on a dice.
- Small groups (up to about four or five) can be seen without counting.
- Seeing two and two together makes four.
- When we do count, we touch each thing only once.

WHAT HAPPENS IN THIS CASE
In Mabel's veggie patch, Mabel has painted a garden stepping stone to look like a ladybird. Ivy says how many dots it has straight away, and Otto thinks that's not fair because she didn't count. The narrator explains that at a glance means one quick look, like the dots on a dice. Otto counts the dots and gets five. Ivy saw four at a glance, and Otto thinks she guessed. Detective Bramble says, "Let's not guess. Let's test." Your child is asked to point to the dots, then to choose five dots or four dots, with five quiet seconds to think. Two dots on this wing and two dots on that wing make four: it's four! Otto realises he touched one dot twice. Touching each dot only once proves it a second way: one, two, three, four. Case solved! Then a real ladybird lands on Otto's nose and he asks it to count. At the end, your child is invited to say the rule out loud: "You can see small groups without counting."

KEY WORDS
at a glance, quick look, dots, how many, two, four, count, touch once, ladybird, dice

TRY IT AT HOME
Roll a dice and say the number fast, without counting. Then try dot cards, dominoes or a few pegs held up for one second. Start with groups of one to four, and ask "How did you see it?" (for example, "two and two").
