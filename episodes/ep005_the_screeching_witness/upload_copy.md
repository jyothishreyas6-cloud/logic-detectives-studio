FOR GROWN-UPS
Learning goal: know that a synonym is a word that means nearly the same, and choose a more exact synonym to make a description clearer, using the rule "Swap a dull word for a more exact synonym." (so "loud" becomes "screeching", which tells us which bird it was).
Curriculum link: NSW English K-10 Syllabus (2022), Stage 2 (Years 3 and 4), Vocabulary, EN2-VOCAB-01: "builds knowledge and use of Tier 1, Tier 2 and Tier 3 vocabulary through interacting, wide reading and writing, and by defining and analysing words". https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/learning-areas/english/english-k-10-2022/outcomes
Ask your child after watching: "Why did 'screeching' help more than 'loud'?"
Try it together: find a better word for "nice" (a nice friend is kind; a nice lamington is delicious).

"Hey! That's not fair! My lamington is gone!" Which big, loud bird took it?

WHAT YOUR CHILD WILL LEARN
- A synonym is a word that means nearly the same thing (hungry and starving).
- Dull words like "big" and "loud" fit lots of things, so they don't help a detective.
- Swap a dull word for a more exact synonym: "loud" becomes "screeching".

WHAT HAPPENS IN THIS CASE
At the school fete cake stall, Otto's lamington has gone from the plate. Pip the mouse saw it happen and wrote down: "It was big. It was loud." Two big, loud birds sit on the fence: a kookaburra and a cockatoo. Otto blames the kookaburra straight away, but Ivy notices the cockatoo is big and loud too. The narrator explains synonyms with a sausage sizzle: hungry and starving mean nearly the same, but starving is more exact. Detective Bramble says, "Let's not guess. Let's test." Your child is asked to point to the two big birds, then Pip swaps "loud" for "screeching" and your child chooses between the kookaburra and the cockatoo with five quiet seconds to think. Kookaburras laugh; cockatoos screech. A closer look finds coconut crumbs on the cockatoo's beak. Case solved! Then the kookaburra laughs, glad nobody blamed it. At the end, your child is invited to say the rule out loud: "Swap a dull word for a more exact synonym."

KEY WORDS
synonym, dull word, exact, witness, describe, loud, screeching, hungry, starving

TRY IT AT HOME
Pick a dull word like "nice", "good" or "big". Take turns finding more exact synonyms for different things: a nice friend (kind), a nice lamington (delicious), a big dog (huge, enormous). Then describe something in the room using the most exact word you can, and see if the other person can guess what it is.
