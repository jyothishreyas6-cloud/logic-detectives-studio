FOR GROWN-UPS
Learning goal: half means two pieces that are exactly the same size, and you can find the middle of something long by folding it end to end.
Curriculum link: NSW Mathematics K-10 Syllabus (2022), Stage 1 (Years 1 and 2), Geometric measure: subdividing lengths to find halves, and measuring length in centimetres.
Ask your child after watching: "How could we check if two pieces are really halves?"
Try it together: fold a strip of paper end to end, cut at the fold, and line the two pieces up.

Is this really half? Otto says, "Hey! That's not fair!" Today's case is all about what "half" means.

WHAT YOUR CHILD WILL LEARN
- Half means two pieces that are exactly the same size.
- To find the middle of something long, fold it end to end.
- You can check halves by lining them up or measuring.

WHAT HAPPENS IN THIS CASE
We start with a lamington shared between two friends, so each gets exactly the same amount. Then Otto wants to cut one ribbon into two kite tails at his pencil mark, but Ivy wonders if the mark is really in the middle. Detective Bramble says, "Let's not guess. Let's test." Your child hears two choices and gets five quiet seconds to think, and Bramble asks them to point to the middle. Bramble folds the ribbon end to end and finds the true middle: Otto's mark was a little too far. They cut at the fold, line the pieces up and measure 10 cm and 10 cm. Case solved! At the end, your child is invited to say the rule out loud: "Half means two equal parts."

KEY WORDS
half, halves, equal, same size, same length, middle, fold, measure, centimetres

TRY IT AT HOME
Give your child a piece of string or a strip of paper. Ask them to find half without a ruler by folding it end to end, then cut or mark the fold and measure both pieces to check they match. You can also share a lamington, a sandwich or a piece of fruit and ask, "Are these really halves?"
