FOR GROWN-UPS
Learning goal: hear that spoken words are made of beats called syllables, and count them by clapping, using the rule "Clap once for each syllable." (Ot-to has two claps; Pip has one).
Curriculum link: NSW English K-10 Syllabus (2022), Early Stage 1 (Kindergarten), Phonological awareness, ENE-PHOAW-01: "identifies, blends, segments and manipulates phonological units in spoken words as a strategy for reading and creating texts". https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/learning-areas/english/english-k-10-2022/outcomes
Ask your child after watching: "How many claps are in your name?"
Try it together: clap your own name, then the names of everyone in the family.

"Oh no! Which bucket is mine?" The tags have dots, not names.

WHAT YOUR CHILD WILL LEARN
- A syllable is a beat in a word (lam-ing-ton has three).
- Clap once for each syllable to count the beats.
- Names have beats too: Ot-to has two claps and Pip has one.

WHAT HAPPENS IN THIS CASE
At the beach, two buckets look just the same. Each tag has dots, not names: one dot and two dots. Otto can't tell which bucket is his. The narrator explains that a syllable is a beat in a word, like lam-ing-ton: three claps. Otto rushes in: "One dot! O for Otto! Mine!" But Ivy notices Pip has a bucket too. Detective Bramble says, "Let's not guess. Let's test." Your child is asked to show two claps, then to choose which tag has Otto's claps, with five quiet seconds to think. Clapping the names proves it: Ot-to has two claps and Pip has one, so the two-dot tag is Otto's. Flipping the tag over shows the name Otto on the back. Case solved! Then Otto claps "Detective Bramble" (five claps) and his paws are tired. At the end, your child is invited to say the rule out loud: "Clap once for each syllable."

KEY WORDS
syllable, beat, clap, name, dot, tag, one, two, three

TRY IT AT HOME
Clap your own name, one clap for each beat. Then clap the names of family, pets and favourite foods (Veg-e-mite, lam-ing-ton). Sort them: who has one clap, two claps, three claps? Whose name has the most?
