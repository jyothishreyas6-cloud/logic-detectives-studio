FOR GROWN-UPS
Learning goal: count a collection to ten by saying one number for each object, touching or moving each one only once, and know that the last number said tells how many.
Curriculum link: NSW Mathematics K-10 Syllabus (2022), Early Stage 1 (Kindergarten), Representing whole numbers, MAE-RWN-01: "demonstrates an understanding of how whole numbers indicate quantity". https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/learning-areas/mathematics/mathematics-k-10-2022/outcomes
Ask your child after watching: "How did Otto get eleven when he only had ten?"
Try it together: count ten pegs into a cup, one at a time, saying one number for each peg.

"Hey! That's not fair! Otto got more!" Does Otto really have more acorns?

WHAT YOUR CHILD WILL LEARN
- Counting tells us how many.
- One number goes with each thing, and each thing is counted only once.
- The last number we say tells how many.

WHAT HAPPENS IN THIS CASE
On the back verandah, Otto and Ivy have each gathered acorns. Otto counts his messy ring of acorns very fast and gets "eleven!", so Ivy says it's not fair. At a sausage sizzle, one sausage goes on each bread, and counting works the same way: one number for each thing. Detective Bramble says, "Let's not guess. Let's test." Your child is asked to point to each acorn, then chooses between "Otto has more" and "They have the same" with five quiet seconds to think. Each acorn moves into a ten frame, one number at a time, and the last number is ten. Otto counted his first acorn twice! Making pairs proves it a second way: every acorn has a partner. Case solved! Then Otto tries to count Bramble's spines (about five thousand... maybe tomorrow). At the end, your child is invited to say the rule out loud: "The last number tells how many."

KEY WORDS
count, how many, one number for each, ten frame, the same, more, pairs

TRY IT AT HOME
Count ten pegs (or pasta pieces, or blocks) into a cup, one at a time, saying one number for each. Then tip them out in a messy pile and count again: did you still get ten? Make pairs with two piles to check if they are the same.
