FOR GROWN-UPS
Learning goal: count forwards and backwards by ones past 100 to find a missing number, and know the rule "Past one hundred, keep counting by ones." (so 99, 100, 101, 102, not 99, 100, 200).
Curriculum link: NSW Mathematics K-10 Syllabus (2022), Stage 1 (Years 1 and 2), Representing whole numbers, MA1-RWN-01: "applies an understanding of place value and the role of zero to read, write and order two- and three-digit numbers". https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/learning-areas/mathematics/mathematics-k-10-2022/outcomes
Ask your child after watching: "Why isn't the missing number two hundred?"
Try it together: on a walk, read the house numbers out loud and say which number should come next.

"Oh no! Our cabin lost its number!" Which number is missing?

WHAT YOUR CHILD WILL LEARN
- Counting on means saying the next number.
- Past one hundred, keep counting by ones: 99, 100, 101, 102.
- Counting back (104, 103, 102) is a second way to check.

WHAT HAPPENS IN THIS CASE
At the beach caravan park, the holiday cabins are numbered 99, 100, 101, then a blank sign, then 103 and 104. Otto says the missing number is "two hundred!", counting "ninety-nine, one hundred, two hundred". But Ivy notices that cabin already says 101. Counting on is like a cricket scoreboard: one more run, one more number. Detective Bramble says, "Let's not guess. Let's test." Your child is asked to point to the empty sign, then chooses between 200 and 102 with five quiet seconds to think. Counting on from 99 lands on 102, and counting back from 104 lands on 102 too. Case solved! Then a snail moves in next to cabin 102 (it brought its own house). At the end, your child is invited to say the rule out loud: "Past one hundred, keep counting by ones."

KEY WORDS
count on, count back, next number, one hundred, one hundred and one, one hundred and two, missing number

TRY IT AT HOME
Go for a walk and read house numbers out loud. Cover one number on a letterbox with your hand and ask which number it is. Count on past 100 together (98, 99, 100, 101, 102), then count back down again.
