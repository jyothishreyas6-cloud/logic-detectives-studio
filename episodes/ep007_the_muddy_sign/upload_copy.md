FOR GROWN-UPS
Learning goal: know that a digraph is two letters that make one sound (sh, ch), and read them as one sound when sounding out a word, using the rule "Two letters can make one sound." (s and h in "shop" say sh, so shop has four letters but three sounds).
Curriculum link: NSW English K-10 Syllabus (2022), Stage 1 (Years 1 and 2), Phonic knowledge, EN1-PHOKW-01: "uses initial and extended phonics, including vowel digraphs, trigraphs to decode and encode words when reading and creating texts". https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/learning-areas/english/english-k-10-2022/outcomes
Ask your child after watching: "Which two letters make the sh sound?"
Try it together: hunt for sh words around the house (shoe, shell, brush) and say the sh sound as one sound.

WHAT YOUR CHILD WILL LEARN
- A digraph is two letters that make one sound.
- S and h say sh, like fish. C and h say ch, like chips.
- Sounding out each letter on its own does not work for a digraph: s-h-o-p is "shop", not "suh-huh-op".
- Counting sounds: shop has four letters but three sounds (sh, o, p).

WHAT HAPPENS IN THIS CASE
At Mabel's farm stall on a country lane, the morning after rain, mud has splashed over Mabel's new sign. Otto and Ivy can't read the stall's name. The narrator explains that a digraph means two letters, one sound: s and h say sh, like fish, and c and h say ch, like chips. Pip the mouse wipes the mud away, and Otto rushes to sound out every letter: "Suh-huh-op!" Ivy notices that isn't a word. Detective Bramble says, "Let's not guess. Let's test." Your child is asked to show the sh sound, then to choose what the sign says, with five quiet seconds to think. Reading s and h together as one sound gives "Shop": Mabel's Shop. Counting the sounds proves it a second way, with a line under sh and a dot under o and p: four letters, three sounds. Case solved! Then Otto tries ch, ch, ch and sounds like a train. At the end, your child is invited to say the rule out loud: "Two letters can make one sound."

KEY WORDS

TRY IT AT HOME
Hunt for sh words around the house: shoe, shirt, shell, brush, dish. Say each one slowly and listen for the sh sound. Then try ch words: chair, chips, lunch. Write one word and draw a line under the two letters that make one sound.
